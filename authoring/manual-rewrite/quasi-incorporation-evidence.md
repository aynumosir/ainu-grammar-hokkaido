## Terminology, wordhood, and person marking

| Material claim | Source with exact locator | Agreement, difference, or limitation | Editorial treatment | Corpus evidence status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 連他動詞 consists of two adjacent words with a coherent verbal meaning, often a locative noun plus transitive verb. | Tamura 1997, p. 15, §C.4 | Tamura expressly describes the noun as the following verb's object. Its person marking is O-series; the verb's is A-series. She reports that Kindaichi and Chiri instead treated the expression as one verb with an infixed subject marker. | Name Tamura's term explicitly and explain the two-word analysis. Preserve the historical difference in segmentation; do not imply that all descriptions already share Satō's lexical-word terminology. | Source description and examples; no new corpus test. |
| Satō's verbal QI corresponds to the traditional “third class” and Nakagawa's 連動詞. | Satō 2021, pp. 89–90, §5; Satō 2016, §3, pp. 89–90; Tamura 1997, p. 15 | Satō 2021 explicitly cites Chiri 1974/1936:84 and Nakagawa 1995:7 at this point. The direct connection to Tamura's 連他動詞 is independently supported by her matching structural description and overlapping lexical examples. The paper does not claim that Tamura originated the class. | Present these as related labels for the verbal pattern. Avoid translating 連動詞 here as “serial verbs”: the relevant examples contain a noun and one verb, not a series of verbs. | Terminological comparison, not corpus confirmation. |
| Two morphological words can form one lexical word. | Satō 2021, pp. 93–94; Satō 2022, §6 | “Lexical word” is Satō's proposed category for conventional semantic units suitable for dictionary listing. It includes morphological words and idiomatic phrases. It does not assert phonological fusion. | Explain the two senses of “word” before using QI to discuss `or ta`. Do not use orthographic spacing alone as proof of lexical independence or fusion. | Source analysis. |
| Inflectional possessor marking and derivational prefixes behave differently. | Satō 2016, p. 89, exx. 8–9; Satō 2022, §4, exx. 25–29; Satō 2021, p. 90, exx. 21–22 | `*a-i-par-oyki` is rejected with inflectional `i-` indexing the literary first person; `i-par a-oyki` is the phrasal alternative. Derivational `i-par-oyki` ‘support someone’ and `yay-par-oyki` ‘support oneself’ are morphological words. The identical spelling of the two `i-` forms does not make them the same morpheme. | Keep inflectional and derivational `i-` separate. Do not say possessor-requiring object incorporation is impossible without specifying the inflectionally marked construction at issue. | Published grammaticality contrast; no new elicitation. |
| Derivational `u-`, `yay-`, `i-` can turn the verbal phrase into one word. | Tamura 1997, p. 15, §C.4, `u-tom-osma`, `u-tom-osma-re`, `a-u-tóm-osma-re` | Directly agrees with Satō's distinction between derivational marking and person indexing. Tamura illustrates `u-`; her statement also names `yay-` and `i-`. | Useful bridge between the verb-word template and QI. Avoid constructing additional unattested paradigms. | Published description and examples. |
| Basic noun form plus an O-series prefix is a central diagnostic, but long-form selection is not a uniform ban. | Satō 2021, pp. 90–94; Tamura 1997, p. 15 | Satō contrasts `en-par` with ordinary possessive `ku-paro`. Tamura explicitly says long forms depend on the kind and presence of the O marker and gives `e-tom k-osma` alongside **`eci-tomó k-osma`**. The latter long form is visible in the scan. | Do not generalize the Chitose `en-par` contrast to “every overt O prefix always selects a short form.” Keep Tamura's Saru forms and Satō's Chitose analysis attributed. | Independent published Saru and Chitose descriptions; no harmonized cross-dialect paradigm. |
| An overt possessor NP and an affiliative noun can still participate in lexical QI. | Satō 2021, pp. 90–91, ex. 23; pp. 93–94; note 16 | `a-kor acapo φ-par-o a-oyki kus ne na` retains `paro` and the meaning ‘support’. Satō still treats it as a lexical word, although morphologically a phrase. Note 16 leaves parts of the case/coindexing analysis open and distinguishes optional emphatic pronouns from ordinary possessor NPs. | Do not define QI as obligatorily lacking affiliative morphology. Show the external-NP counterpart and mark the explanatory account as Satō's proposal. | Published example; local corpus match not sought. |
| Tightness includes restrictions on interpolation and conventional meaning. | Satō 2016, pp. 89–90, §3; Satō 2022, §4, immediately before ex. 29 | The 2016 wording says adverbials cannot be inserted freely; 2022 says no element can be inserted. The local passages do not present an exhaustive series of insertion tests. | Describe restricted separation and lexical conventionality. Do not turn the stronger summary into a newly verified universal ban on every particle or adverb in every member of the class. | Published characterization; no fresh distributional tests. |
| Semantic translations differ across descriptions without establishing structural disagreement. | Tamura 1997, p. 15; Satō 2022, §6, exx. 32–37; Tamura 1996, `par(o) oyki` | Tamura's `ka(si) opas` is running to visit someone ill; Satō's `ka opas` is ‘save’. Tamura's `tom(o) oytak` is soothing; Satō gives persuading. `par(o) oyki` in the dictionary emphasizes feeding/supporting parents or older people. | Keep example translations tied to their source rather than assembling a single supposedly exhaustive inventory with universal meanings. | Lexical source evidence; no frequency comparison. |

## Satō 2021: `or` sequences and the extension of QI

| Material claim | Source with exact locator | Agreement, difference, or limitation | Editorial treatment | Corpus evidence status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `or` makes a nominal referent available as a grammatical location. | Satō 2021, §§2–3, pp. 82–87; Tamura 1996, `or` | Compatible with established locative-noun descriptions. Satō considers this insufficient by itself to explain O-series marking and the distribution of `or` versus `oro`. | Preserve the ordinary place-reading account, then introduce the unresolved morphosyntactic questions. | Published examples only. |
| `or ta` can be analyzed as a two-word lexical unit parallel to `par oyki`. | Satō 2021, pp. 91–94, table 2 and ensuing discussion | `en-or ta` and `en-par oyki` both combine O-series marking with a basic noun form. The proposed promotion of the possessor to the complex's object explains the shared marking in Satō's account. Case particles are not thereby asserted to be transitive verbs. | Explicit attribution is necessary: this is the paper's central new hypothesis, not a settled definition of all locative nouns. | Author's analysis; not an independently verified corpus inference. |
| `cise or ta` is proposed to contain incorporation into a QI unit. | Satō 2021, p. 94 and note 22, continuing p. 95 | Preferred exposition: `cise-or ta`, with `cise` incorporated into `or ta`. Alternative: `cise-or-ta`, replacing derivational `i-` in `i-y-or ta`. Note 22 also considers two QI relations. A rare specific NP, `tan suy or un iteki ahup an`, complicates an ordinary NI analysis. | Preserve competing segmentations as analyses. Do not respell all source examples with hyphens as though the morphological structure were established. | Source discussion; no new acceptability or accent test. |
| Animacy conditions the ordinary `or`/`oro` contrast, with exceptions. | Satō 2021, pp. 85–86, exx. 11–15; pp. 88–89, exx. 17–20; pp. 92–93, table 2 and notes 18–20 | Ordinary human/animate NPs favor `oro`. Bare `or` occurs with generalized gods/humans/Japanese people and `isa` ‘doctor’, which Satō interprets as abstract or occupational readings. Bear-agent examples include both `or wa` and `oro wa`. | Do not state an absolute animate/inanimate rule. Explain the representative contrast and retain examples that limit it. | Attested examples in the paper; their proposed semantic explanation remains an analysis. |
| Complex inanimate NPs can occur before `oro`. | Satō 2021, p. 88, ex. 20; pp. 91–93, notes 17 and 20 | `poronno an pe oro wa` ‘from among many things’ is an explicit exception to table 2's simplified × cell. The paper does not claim that complex inanimate NPs invariably require `oro`. | Include the exception or qualify the tendency. Do not present the table's × symbols as a complete grammaticality paradigm. | Published exceptional example; no new count. |
| Several starred forms are theoretically expected but unattested in the author's material. | Satō 2021, pp. 92–93, explanations 4–8; p. 96, note 25 | Examples include `*cip kes anpa`, `*aynu kes anpa`, `*cip kese anpa`, and hypotheses such as `*kamuy-kes-anpa-an`. Much of the wording is “no example has been found,” not a reported elicited rejection. | Label these as unattested/hypothetical where discussed. Do not reuse the star without explaining its evidentiary status. | Negative search observations reported by the author; not a new local-corpus search. |
| Locative QI and verbal QI are not fully parallel. | Satō 2021, p. 91 before table 2; pp. 95–96, especially the discussion preceding note 25 | `cise or ta` permits the inanimate noun plus basic locative pattern; the corresponding noun-plus-basic-noun pattern is not found with verbal QI. The paper calls this a substantial unresolved difference and offers only tentative explanations. | Retain this limit when presenting the comparison. A table showing only the shared properties would overstate the result. | Explicit unresolved issue in the source. |
| Conventional lexical restrictions matter. | Satō 2021, pp. 95–96, including notes 23–25 | The proposed lexical units have restricted combinations and customary meanings; the paper does not license every possible noun with every locative expression. Human NI is described as very rare, with counterexamples mentioned, rather than logically impossible. | Avoid a productive rule generating new `or` or verbal-QI combinations without source evidence. | Source observations; no exhaustive lexical inventory established. |
| `orke` and A-series marking are outside the demonstrated core. | Satō 2021, §1, p. 81; p. 94, note 21 | `orke` is set aside because the author has only three examples. Possible A-series locative-noun forms are explicitly left for further research. | Do not extend this paper's proposal to a complete `or/oro/orke` paradigm or cite it as proof of regular A-series alternatives. | Stated scope limits. |
| Surface `or` versus `oro` may be hard to identify. | Satō 2021, p. 91, note 17 | The author notes ambiguity unless clear accent/pronunciation separates the forms; unambiguous `oro ta` is especially useful. | Preserve source spelling; avoid numerical counts from unverified normalized strings. | Phonological limitation acknowledged by source; recordings not checked in this audit. |
